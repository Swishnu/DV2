Pokémon Trading Cards: 1999 to 2026

How the Pokémon card game has grown since it came to Australia in 1999, and where Australia fits in.

1. How many cards have been printed?

Pokémon cards came out in Australia in 1999. Since then, 85 billion cards have been printed around the world. Most of these are recent: about 60% were printed in just the last 5 years.

Some terms used on this page

Set: a group of cards released together, e.g. Base Set (1999).

Rarity: how hard a card is to find in a pack. Common cards are in every pack, the rarest ones are very hard to get.

Type: every Pokémon has a type, such as Fire, Water or Grass.

2. Sets are getting bigger

A new set of cards comes out every few months. Sets used to have about 100 cards each. Many recent sets have over 200 once you count the "secret" cards, which are numbered past the end of the set (e.g. card 105/102).

3. More rare cards

In 1999 there were only three rarities: Common, Uncommon and Rare. Now there are lots of "special" rare cards, like shiny, full art and illustration cards. Today about 4 in every 10 new cards are special rares, and normal Rares have become less common.

4. Rarer cards cost more

Rarer cards sell for a lot more. A typical Common card sells for about 15 US cents, while a typical Special illustration rare sells for about US$34.

5. Pokémon around the world

The best players compete at the World Championships ("Worlds") every year. Compared to its population, Australia sends about twice as many players as the United States.

Notes: Hong Kong and Singapore are too small to show on the map but are in the bar chart. Countries with only 1 or 2 players are not in the top 15. Data by country is only available from 2025 onwards.

To qualify for Worlds, players earn points at official tournaments around the year. Australia hosts a few of these every season.

Note: Japan and some other Asian countries run their own tournament series, so their events are not in this data.
