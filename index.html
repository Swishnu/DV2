Gotta Print 'Em All

Pokémon cards went from a 1999 schoolyard craze to 85 billion printed, and some single cards now sell for over US$1,000. So why are some cards so expensive? This page follows the cards, their prices and the players around the world, including Australia.

85 billion cards and counting

Pokémon cards came out in Australia in 1999. Since then, 85 billion cards have been printed around the world. Most of these are recent: about 60% were printed in just the last five years. That growth came from two places: more sets, and bigger ones.

Set: a group of cards released together, e.g. Base Set (1999).

Rarity: how hard a card is to find in a pack. Common cards are in every pack; the rarest ones are very hard to get.

Sets keep getting bigger

A new set of cards comes out every few months. Sets used to have about 100 cards each. Many recent sets have over 200 once you count the secret cards, which are numbered past the end of the set (e.g. card 105/102). Most of those extra cards are not ordinary ones: they are the rarest in the set.

Rare cards are taking over

In 1999 there were only three rarities: Common, Uncommon and Rare. Now there are many special rare cards, such as shiny, full art and illustration cards. Today about 4 in every 10 new cards are special rares, and normal Rares have become less common.

Rarer means pricier

Rarer cards sell for a lot more. A typical Common card sells for about 15 US cents, while a typical Special illustration rare sells for about US$34. Prices also spread out a lot within each rarity: a couple of Commons sell for over US$300.

Rarity isn't the whole story; age matters too. Buying one of every card from the 2003 set Aquapolis costs about US$17,400, while a typical Scarlet & Violet set costs about US$1,200.

A world game, with Aussies in it

The cards aren't only collected, they're played. The best players meet at the World Championships ("Worlds") every year. Compared to its population, Australia sends about twice as many players as the United States.

To qualify for Worlds, players earn points at official tournaments around the year. Australia hosts a few of these every season.

For Australians, getting to Worlds means one of the longest trips of any country.

Where has Worlds been held?

Worlds has been held in the United States 18 out of 21 times and has never been in Australia. In 2027 it will be in Singapore, the closest it has ever been to Australia. (No Worlds was held in person in 2020 or 2021.)

Still growing

Worlds is getting bigger, and so is the game in Australia. Australia's team grew from 18 to 21 players between 2025 and 2026.

Back home, Australian tournaments are growing too. The Melbourne Regional in May 2026 had 959 players, three times as many as Perth in December 2024.

So why are some cards so expensive?

The data points to three things working together. Far more cards are printed than ever before, but a growing share of each set is made up of special and secret rares that are hard to pull from a pack. Those rare cards sell for hundreds of times more than ordinary ones, and older cards cost more again. Meanwhile the game keeps attracting players, from the 959 people at the Melbourne Regional to the 797 at Worlds 2026. More cards have not made the best cards any easier to get.
